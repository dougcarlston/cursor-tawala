## Message to Claude

You are a product-and-go-to-market strategist. I need a **marketing landscape and a wedge**, not a launch campaign and not a homepage design.

I built a platform called **Tawala**. I am deciding which **audiences and jobs** should pull the public toward it. Those choices will decide which apps we build next, how the Library is organized, and what the Home page features. The Home page will change a lot if a category starts attracting real attention. I do not want Home to *be* the Library; I also do not want a generic SaaS landing page.

Be concrete. Disagree with me where the evidence is thin. Prefer one sharp thesis over a balanced “all of the above.” Do not recommend becoming Airtable, Notion, Typeform, Kahoot, Canvas, or Strava. Tell me where Tawala actually wins, and where it should refuse to compete.

### What Tawala is

Tawala lets ordinary people use the web and email to **organize a group and keep records**, without programming.

A Tawala **Project** is not “a form.” It is:

- **Forms** — collect answers (text, blanks, multiple choice, files, hidden fields, skip logic).
- **Documents** — show, print, or email results (letters, reports, dashboards, confirmation pages).
- **Process** — if / for-each / set / send email / append records. The useful magic is **database + email + notification** in one shareable app.

Two kinds of people:

1. **Most people never open the Designer.** They Test Drive a Library app, Copy it into **My Tawala**, customize a few labels and questions, then share a link (email or their own site).
2. **A small minority** (historically we guessed ~5%) use **Designer** to author new Projects from scratch.

We already learned the hard way: showing people the Process editor makes most of them glaze over. **Customizable ready-made apps** are the public product. Designer is how those apps get born, and how power users go further. Customizables are also much harder to build well than blank templates — do not recommend a hundred shallow templates.

**How a stranger meets it (intended loop):**

1. No account required to **Test Drive** (a private try-out; other visitors do not see what you type).
2. Test Drive links are **shareable** (viral try, not “here is my live class roster”).
3. Soft gate: you make an account when you want to **keep data** about yourself or your people — not after N free clicks.
4. **Library** = public catalog of vetted apps. **My Tawala** = your deployed copies. **Publish** = deliberate bridge from private copy into the public Library (vetted; not a junk drawer).
5. Email is integral (invites, nudges, confirmations) and **costs us money**, so volume will eventually be billed. Do not assume infinite free blast campaigns.

Tawala is **not** a website builder, not an LMS/SIS, not a general no-code database. A “class tracker” or a “fitness log” would be a **Project running on Tawala**, not a new company.

### History that should constrain you

- Deepest real deployment was **youth sports league operations** (registration, rosters, team assignment, parent/coach dashboards, reminder email) — especially Bay Area leagues. That is “go deep in one operational domain,” not “a form widget.”
- An earlier “anyone can build any process” story did not explain itself. Templates and examples helped; the blank Designer did not.
- We are **rebuilding** Designer in the browser and standing the public site back up. The Library is small on purpose. Do not treat the current catalog as the market.

### What exists today (honest, small)

Live or nearly live Library apps are **instruments and gatherings**, not vertical products:

- Simple and multi-question **surveys**; a **single-question poll** (admin vs respondent are different doors).
- **Sign-up sheet**.
- **Get Together** and **Potluck** (event coordination).
- **Shared To-Do**.
- **Online Exam Builder** — a teacher (or anyone) copies it, writes questions, runs an exam. Version 2 will let authors **Publish the exam as content** under the Builder (keep questions/setup, strip student answers). Other teachers copy that exam and run it with *their* classes. This “Builder + user-contributed content” pattern can repeat; Exam Builder is the first.
- A light **trivia/test** sample (Horses and Penguins).
- Sports/registration history exists in the archive (DirtBowl, MVSC, CYO) but is **not** the public Library story yet.
- **Automated List Builder** (invitation-heavy) stays private until public invite-confirm links work.

**Missing on purpose or not built:** a year-long **class tracker** (all of a teacher’s students, and everything that must be tracked across a year). That is an obvious teacher need sitting next to Exam Builder. Fitness, diet, HR suites, chain-letter games — none of these are products yet.

Library category labels today are leftover buckets (Activities, Meetings, Polls, Sports, Business, Entertainment, Advanced). They are **not** a marketing thesis.

### My hunches (challenge these)

**Teachers.** Good market. We have one strong project (Exam Builder). The other obvious need is a **class tracker**: the teacher’s students, and the year’s worth of things to record about them. Teachers already share materials; the Version 2 “publish an exam, other teachers copy it” path fits how they work. I do not want Tawala to become an LMS.

**HR.** Another audience that pays real money for tools of this general sort. Also a **hard** market (procurement, security, incumbents). Some of our categories may still resonate (surveys, sign-ups, reviews, onboarding checklists) without “selling HR.”

**Entertainment / viral.** Chain-letter-style games, trivia contests, and similar can go viral **if they are nowhere near the serious apps**. Mixing a joke poll with a teacher’s exam or an HR-ish workflow on the same storefront would cheapen the serious side.

**Personal improvement.** Large appetite: physical fitness, skill-building (languages, certifications, career skills), diet, weight, memory. People like to **test and record progress over time** — that is a Tawala-shaped job (forms + history + maybe email nudges). The hard part is a **central marketing thesis** for “personal improvement” as a aisle. It is easier to pick **one** area and go as deep as possible. I live in California; **personal fitness** is an obsession here. I would rather go deep in fitness than offer a shallow self-help mall.

**Home page.** I expect it to change as eyeballs appear. Featured apps on Home are a **storefront window**, not the catalog. I need a landscape so we know which window to put in the window — and which projects are worth building so the window is not a lie.

### What I need from you

Write a planning memo with these sections. Use short declarative prose. No slogans, no growth-hacker checklists, no “community and content marketing” filler.

1. **One-sentence job of Tawala** for a stranger who will never see Designer. Then 3–5 sentences of what that sentence *excludes*.

2. **Landscape, not a list of personas.** Map 6–10 **jobs-to-be-done** that actually fit Forms + records-over-time + email (teachers, HR fragments, fitness, viral games, clubs/leagues, churches, etc.). For each job: who feels the pain weekly, whether Test Drive is enough or they need year-long data, whether they will Copy/Publish, and whether they belong on Home, in a serious Library aisle, or in a **separate entertainment channel**.

3. **Wedge recommendation.** Pick **one** audience × one job × one proof-app to go deep on for the next public chapter. Rank my hunches (teachers / HR / viral entertainment / California fitness / other you introduce). Say what we would **stop featuring** on Home if we pick that wedge. Give the strongest case *against* your pick.

4. **Teachers specifically.** If Exam Builder is the beachhead, is a **class tracker** the right second app, or is that a trap (SIS gravity, year-long data, privacy)? What is the smallest tracker that is still “the year’s students,” and what must the platform already support (rosters that persist, privacy, school-year rollover, parent email)? How should teacher **content** (exams) sit in the Library vs teacher **operations** (the tracker)?

5. **Viral vs serious.** Propose a **hard separation** (different Home, subdomain, Library section, tone) so chain-letter/trivia can grow without sitting next to exams and sign-ups. Tell me if viral should be delayed until the serious aisle has a face.

6. **Fitness as a deep vertical.** What would “go as deep as possible in personal fitness” mean as Tawala Projects (not as a wearable company)? What is the first proof-app? What is the 2nd and 3rd so it is a **line**, not a single BMI form? Where does it overlap teachers (PE) or clubs, and where must it stay consumer?

7. **HR.** Either a narrow on-ramp that does not say “HR,” or a clear “do not chase this until X.” Do not outline an HR suite.

8. **Implications for product development.** For the recommended wedge, list the **next 5–8 Projects or Builders** in build order, and the **platform capabilities** each one depends on (examples: year-long records, invitation confirm on a public host, Builder publish-as-content, email nudges, shared data across a class). Mark which are marketing-critical vs nice. Call out anything that would force Designer work vs Library-app work.

9. **Home and Library IA.** Given that Home is a changing window: what 4 featured slots should it show **before** we know if the wedge is working, and what replaces them **after**? How should Library aisles be named if we accept a serious / entertainment split? Do not design CSS.

10. **What would falsify the plan** in a private-beta (go live but not the open internet)? 5 observables. If those fail, what is the fallback wedge?

Do not invent a pricing page. Do not tell me to hire a CMO. Do not recommend ratings/stars. Do not expand Designer as the growth loop. The growth loop is: try → keep a copy → share with a group → (later) publish content others can copy.

If you are unsure, say so and give the experiment that would resolve it.
