# OEB teacher validation — talk after they have used it

Used with `TAWALA_WEDGE_ACTION_PLAN.md`. Oral, one teacher at a time. **No interview until they have built an exam or quiz and tried it with some of their students.**

People will try to please you. Sequence and wording below are meant to keep that down. You will still hear it; the open narrative at the start is the main defense.

---

## Three beats (do not collapse them)

**1. Recruit call (short).** Willing to try? Enough **calendar window** for busy people (you set this; they are not a weekend homework). Three links will follow. You will **call again after they have used it** — not a survey in the packet. Do not sell Tawala. Do not mention Library or My Tawala.

**2. Use (silent).** Packet only: three links + one-page sheet (roster sentence: we have a class list, not in this demo). **No questions, no $100 sheet, no “tell us what you think.”** If they email impressions early, thank them and save it for the interview — don’t start the interview in email.

**3. Interview (oral, after use).** Individually. More than three questions if they have time and heat. Follow **their** interests. If they have not actually given it to students, **do not run the post-use interview** — reschedule or treat it as “didn’t get to students” (still useful, different questions).

---

## Recruit call — keep it small

Aim: yes/no + a window, not a product pitch.

Draft (edit to your voice):

> I’m testing a tool for writing a quiz or exam and giving it online. I’m not asking you to look at a website. If you’re willing, I’d send three links: one to build the quiz, one for students to take it, one for you to review the results. Try building something you’d actually give, and give it to some kids when you can. I know you’re busy — would **the next two weeks** be enough time? I’ll call you afterward and ask what it was like. No form to fill out.

If they ask “what is Tawala / is this for sale / is this the foundation?” — one honest sentence, then back to the try-out. Don’t preview the feature list.

---

## Interview — order matters

Do **not** start with the $100 list. The list teaches them what you wish they’d want.

Suggested order. Stop when they are done; skip what they already answered in the story.

### A. What they did (behavior before opinion)

1. *Walk me through what you actually did, from the first link to finishing with students.*  
   *(If they skip students: “Did any student take it?” If no — stop the “use” interview; ask what got in the way of giving it.)*
2. *How did this compare with how you usually give a quiz or exam?*  
   *(Don’t name Canvas, Google Forms, paper — let them name it.)*
3. *What did students do that surprised you, if anything?*

### B. Unprompted (the three you already care about)

Ask **before** any feature menu. Allow silence.

4. *Is there anything about this that would make you **not** use it?*  
   *(Prefer this to “the one aspect” — “the one” forces a single villain. If they name several, let them. Follow: “Which of those would actually stop you?”)*
5. *If this stayed exactly as you used it, would you give it to another teacher? Why or why not?*  
   *(Less leading than “what could we add so you’d pass it on.” Adding is one path; “I’d pass it as-is” or “I’d never pass it” are both answers. If they only talk add-ons, then: “And if we didn’t add anything?”)*
6. *What would have to be true for you to use this again next term?*  
   *(Neutral: could be fix, add, or “nothing — I’d use it.”)*

### C. $100 — oral, after the open answers

7. *I’m going to name some things we might build later. They were **not** in what you used. You have $100 of our time to divide — including $0 on an item, or all of it on one, or on something I didn’t name. You can also spend it on **fixing** what you already used rather than on new things.*

   Then read the list slowly. Let them allocate out loud. Don’t defend items. Don’t fill silences with “a lot of teachers want pictures…”

**Already in this try-out** (they used these; do not price):

- Question N of M on each item.
- Go back / change answers, then “Are you all finished now?” before scoring.

**Already in the product, not in this try-out** (one sentence, then the list — do not sell):

- Student login from a roster list.

**Our $100 list — not in the try-out; could be added** (owner Word file Sep 18; items 11–12 removed Sep 18 because they shipped in OEBMod2-nav):

1. Save quizzes and individual scores over the **entire term** for full-year record and review.
2. Class **average** on tests and questions; when reviewing a student, where they did better/worse than the class.
3. **Different point values** per question (1 pt easy, 5 pts hard).
4. **Non-automatic grading** (essays), partial credit (e.g. 0–5), teacher **comment** at the end of the question.
5. Automated **forgiveness** for spelling and small errors on fill-in blanks.
6. **Images** in questions and/or choices.
7. Automated **notification** that the exam is available and how long they have to finish.
8. **Save** quizzes/exams for later, or **pass** them to other teachers/students.
9. **Audio** in questions and/or choices.
10. Multiple-choice with **more than one correct** response.
11. Something we didn’t think of: ______

   **Roster / class list:** still **not** priced. If they bring up retyping names: *We already have a class list that can feed the next exam; it wasn’t in this try-out.* Same for student-from-roster login. One sentence. Don’t sell it.

### D. Branch — only if they still have time and heat

Pick from **their** story, not from our hobby horse:

| If they talked about… | You might ask |
|----------------------|----------------|
| Writing questions | *Where did that get tedious or confusing?* |
| Scoring / results | *What did you need to see that you didn’t?* |
| Student link / cheating / devices | *What worried you?* |
| Time | *About how long did setup take vs a quiz you already give?* |
| Privacy / kids’ names | *Anything you wouldn’t put in here?* |
| Subject (art, language, math, English) | *What couldn’t you ask that you needed to ask?* |

Close: *Anything I didn’t ask that I should have?*

## Phrasing we should not use

| Leading | Why | Prefer |
|---------|-----|--------|
| “What could we **add** to make it so useful you’d pass it on?” | Assumes the gap is missing features | B5–B6 as written |
| “Wouldn’t a roster / parent email / audio be helpful?” | Yes-bias | Let them name it; $100 after |
| “Most teachers hate X” | Social proof | Don’t |
| “This is just a demo of a much bigger system” | Invites explore-mode and politeness | Three links. That’s the product for this call |
| Forcing “the **one** thing” | Collapses a real mess into a slogan | “Anything that would make you not use it?” then “what would actually stop you?” |

The $100 list is **inherently** leading (it names our suite). That is acceptable **only after** unprompted B. Don’t email the list with the packet.

---

## What to write down (not a dashboard)

Per teacher, after the call:

- Used with students? Y/N. Rough how many.
- Story in a few lines (what they did).
- Stoppers (their words).
- Pass-along / next term (their words).
- $100 split.
- Anything they named that wasn’t on the list.
- Body language / heat (one line: bored, lit up, polite, annoyed).

Compare across teachers only after several calls. One glowing friend is not legitimacy.
